The verdict

The competitor wins today on a handful of features any shop owner can see in five minutes. Qistas wins on things their phone-only design can't reach. The plan closes the first list in 30 days, then sells the second.

Qistas already wins on

  • Books in the cloud from the first entry, on the phone and in any computer browser, with no emulator PL03PL05
  • Entries nobody can quietly edit, two-step sign-in, no ads on any plan TX10SC02PL13
  • A real dashboard, dark mode, a first-run guide, and the same five languages switched inside the app RP13UX01PL10
  • A five-language website that search engines can index, and an open API CM08SC11

Qistas loses the sale today because

  • It isn't on Google Play, and nobody can pay for Pro yet PL01CM02
  • There are no investors (المستثمرين), no open contracts and no PDF reports to send IN03CT05RP01
  • There's no app lock, no late-payer alert and no in-app account deletion SC01NT01SC04
  • The free plan is half their size: 5 customers against 10 CM05

The three moves

  1. Close every P0 gap in 30 days, starting with the Google Play launch, which needs a 14-day closed test (Sprint 1).
  2. Lead with what a phone-only app can't do: reminders that send themselves, every device on one ledger, and a team without a per-member fee (K1, K2, K10).
  3. Price the yearly plan near their one-time price, offer a free plan twice their size, and import new users' data for them (6.4, K9).

Read this first. Thirteen points in the brief don't match the code or today's Play listings. Three of them change decisions:

  • Storing books in the user's own Google Sheet or database would break the ledger. I recommend exports instead.
  • Google Pay and Apple Pay can't sell Pro inside the apps, because the stores require their own billing.
  • The web version is a Laravel app, not Flutter.

All thirteen are listed with a recommendation in 6.9.

Legal and compliance

AreaWhat to clear, and the plan's stance
Lending rulesQistas is record-keeping software. It never lends, holds or moves customers' money. Some countries license consumer finance or instalment selling (for example SAMA's rules in Saudi Arabia, and Egypt's consumer-finance law). The shop is responsible for its own licence; the terms say so, and no feature turns Qistas into a lender or a collection agency.
Late feesInterest-like charges can be restricted by consumer rules or Sharia practice. Late fees ship switched off, with a charity option and your adviser's wording.
Privacy and data residencySaudi PDPL (enforced since September 2024), the UAE's PDPL and Egypt's Law 151 of 2020 allow transfers abroad under conditions. You'll need a privacy policy per market and data-processing agreements with Supabase and Vercel. A nearer region (6.6) helps both speed and compliance.
Store billing and deletionDigital subscriptions go through store billing inside the apps. Both stores require in-app account deletion, and Google also requires a web deletion link. The Data safety and App Privacy forms must match what the app does.
WhatsAppMeta's messaging and commerce policies limit some financial services. Templates must be utility reminders from the merchant about their own sale, with recorded opt-in and opt-out. Check the current policy before submitting templates.
Signatures and promissory notesSaudi law recognises electronic signatures, but enforceable electronic promissory notes there are issued through the Nafith (نافذ) platform. A note made in Qistas is a record unless integrated with Nafith later. Other countries differ, so your lawyer decides the wording per market.
